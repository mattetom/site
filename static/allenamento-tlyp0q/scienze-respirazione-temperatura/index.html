Respiration and body temperature

Subject: Science Date: 2026-10-28 (notebook "Classification of vertebrates")

Key points

  • All animals breathe: they take in oxygen and release carbon dioxide.
  • Mammals, birds and reptiles breathe using their lungs.
  • Fish breathe with gills. Amphibians: young ones with gills, adults with lungs and skin.
  • Insects breathe through spiracles, small openings connected to tubes called tracheae.
  • Warm-blooded: mammals and birds. Cold-blooded: reptiles, amphibians and fish.

How animals breathe

Fish take water in through the mouth. The water passes over the gills: there oxygen goes into the body and carbon dioxide comes out.

Amphibians change the way they breathe as they grow. The tadpole lives in water and uses gills. The adult frog uses its lungs and its skin. Do not forget either of them!

Insects have no lungs. They have many small openings on their body, the spiracles, connected to a network of small tubes, the tracheae.

Warm-blooded and cold-blooded

Vertebrates are divided into two groups by how they regulate their body temperature.

  • Cold-blooded: the body temperature changes with the environment. That is why a lizard lies in the sun to get warm.
  • Warm-blooded: the body temperature stays the same (constant), whatever the weather is like outside.
