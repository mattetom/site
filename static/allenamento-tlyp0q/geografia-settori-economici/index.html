Economic sectors

Subject: Geography Date: worksheets photographed on 2026-11-15 (no date)

Key points

  • The economy is all the work people do to produce goods (things) and provide services.
  • It is very big and complex, so we divide it into 3 economic sectors.
  • Primary sector: it takes natural resources from the Earth and produces raw materials.
  • Secondary sector: it transforms raw materials into finished goods. Factories and construction.
  • Tertiary sector: it does not produce things, it provides services that help people.

Primary: taking from nature

The primary sector uses natural resources directly from the Earth: - farming (agriculture): growing plants and food; - fishing; - livestock: raising animals for meat, milk and wool; - forestry: cutting down trees for wood; - mineral extraction (mining): coal, oil, gold.

It produces raw materials: things that have not been changed yet. Examples: wood, cotton, wheat.

Secondary: making things

The secondary sector manufactures things and builds: - factories make clothes, cars and machines; - construction builds houses, roads and bridges; - energy, the baker and the craft worker are here too.

When we say industry, we usually mean the secondary sector. Example from the worksheet: wheat → flour → bread. Bread is a finished good: it is ready to be used or sold.

Tertiary: helping people

The tertiary sector provides services: transport (buses, trains, planes), trade (shops, supermarkets), tourism, healthcare (hospitals, doctors), education (teachers, schools) and banking. In developed countries it is the biggest sector, because many people do jobs that help others rather than making things.

The trick

Look at what the person does, not where they work or what they touch: - do they take from nature? Primary; - do they transform or build? Secondary; - do they sell, carry, look after or teach? Tertiary.

Example: Anna raises sheep (primary), makes blankets with the wool (secondary) and sells them to visitors (tertiary). One farm, three sectors. The people who build a hospital are secondary. The driver who carries furniture in a lorry is tertiary.

Check questions

  1. Why do we divide the economy into sectors?
  2. Give three examples of jobs in the primary sector.
  3. What does the secondary sector do with raw materials?
  4. A baker makes bread: which sector? A cashier sells it: which sector?
  5. What is the difference between a raw material and a finished good?
Show the answers

Answers: 1. Because the economy is very big and complex: in sectors it is easier to study. 2. Farmer, fisher, miner, shepherd, logger (any three). 3. It transforms them into finished goods, for example cotton into clothes and iron into cars. 4. The baker is secondary, the cashier is tertiary. 5. A raw material has not been changed yet (wood, cotton); a finished good is ready to be used or sold (a table, a T-shirt).
