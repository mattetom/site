The geographic grid

Subject: Geography Date: 2026-09-17

Key points

  • The geographic grid is a net of imaginary lines drawn on the Earth. They are not real: they help us find places.
  • The horizontal lines are the parallels. There are 180 and they are full circles.
  • The most important parallel is the Equator (0°). It divides the Earth into the Northern Hemisphere and the Southern Hemisphere.
  • The vertical lines are the meridians. There are 360. They go from pole to pole and they are semicircles (half circles).
  • The most important meridian passes through Greenwich, near London: it is the Prime Meridian (0°).

Explanation

To find a place on a map or on a globe, geographers use the geographic grid. It is like the squared paper in your notebook, but drawn on the ball of the Earth.

Parallels are the horizontal lines. They are all parallel to each other and to the Equator. They go all the way round the Earth, but they get smaller near the poles. They tell us how far a place is from the Equator, to the north or to the south.

Meridians are the vertical lines. They go from the North Pole to the South Pole and they are all the same length. They do not go all the way round: they are half circles that meet at the poles.

With these lines every place has two numbers, its geographic coordinates:

  • latitude tells us how far a place is north or south of the Equator. It goes from 0° at the Equator to 90° at the poles. Example: a city at 40° North is 40 degrees above the Equator.
  • longitude tells us how far a place is east or west of Greenwich. It goes from 0° at Greenwich to 180° East and 180° West. Example: a city at 20° East is 20 degrees to the right of Greenwich.

Coordinates are measured in degrees (symbol °). GPS and the maps on a phone use the same system. Example from the worksheet: Buenos Aires is at 34° South and 58° West.

Be careful! Parallels run from east to west (they are horizontal), but they measure north and south. Meridians run from north to south, but they measure east and west.

Words to remember

Word What it means
parallels horizontal lines, 180, full circles
meridians vertical lines, 360, from pole to pole
Equator the parallel at 0°, in the middle of the Earth
Prime Meridian the meridian at 0°, through Greenwich
latitude how far north or south of the Equator, from 0° to 90°
longitude how far east or west of Greenwich, from 0° to 180°
hemisphere half of the Earth

Check questions

  1. How many parallels are there? How many meridians?
  2. Which parallel divides the Earth into the Northern and Southern Hemispheres?
  3. Which town does the Prime Meridian pass through?
  4. A city is at 30° South. Which hemisphere is it in?
  5. Longitude measures the distance from what?
Show the answers

Answers: 1. 180 parallels and 360 meridians. 2. The Equator. 3. Greenwich, near London. 4. The Southern Hemisphere. 5. From the Prime Meridian (Greenwich), to the east or to the west.
