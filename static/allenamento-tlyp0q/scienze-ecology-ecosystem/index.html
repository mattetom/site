Ecology and ecosystem

Subject: Science Date: 2026-09-30

Key points

  • Ecology is the science that studies living organisms, the environment they live in and the relationships between them.
  • A scientist who studies ecology is an ecologist.
  • An ecosystem is made up of three things: living things, non-living things and the relationships between living things and their environment.
  • Examples of ecosystems: forest, pond, meadow, sea, lake, mountain.
  • The two words come from Greek: oikos = home, environment; logos = study; systema = a group of connected parts.

Explanation

Ecology comes from two Greek words: oikos, which means home or environment, and logos, which means study. So ecology is "the study of the home" of living things. It studies living organisms, the environment where they live and the relationships between them.

Ecosystem comes from oikos (home, environment) and systema (a group of connected parts). So an ecosystem is an environment where many parts are connected. There are three parts:

  1. Living things: plants, animals, fungi and microorganisms (living things so small that you can see them only with a microscope).
  2. Non-living things: water, soil, air, light, temperature, rocks.
  3. Relationships: how living things use the environment and how they need each other. For example, a plant needs light and water, the rabbit eats the plant, the fox eats the rabbit.

A trick to tell living from non-living: a living thing is born, grows, feeds and dies. Rocks, water and snow do not: they are non-living, even if they move or change.

Be careful: fungi are living things, even if they are not plants and not animals.

Check questions

  1. What does ecology study?
  2. Which Greek words does "ecology" come from?
  3. What are the three parts of an ecosystem?
  4. Is water a living or a non-living thing? And a mushroom (fungus)?
  5. Write three examples of ecosystems.
Show the answers

Answers: 1. Living organisms, the environment they live in and the relationships between them. 2. Oikos (home, environment) and logos (study). 3. Living things, non-living things, relationships. 4. Water is non-living; a fungus is living. 5. For example forest, pond, meadow.
