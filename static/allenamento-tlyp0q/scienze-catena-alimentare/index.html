The food chain

Subject: Science Date: 2026-09-30

Key points

  • In an ecosystem every living thing needs food: we draw this relationship as a food chain.
  • Each living thing is a link in the chain: it passes matter and energy to the one that eats it.
  • Producers: plants. They make their own food by photosynthesis, using the light of the Sun.
  • Primary consumers eat producers: they are usually herbivores. Secondary consumers eat primary consumers: they are carnivores or omnivores.
  • Decomposers: fungi and bacteria break down dead plants and animals and give the nutrients back to the soil.

Explanation

Everything starts with the Sun (sunlight). Plants use its light to make their food: this is called photosynthesis. That is why plants are the producers: they produce food for everybody.

Then come the consumers. They cannot make their own food, so they must eat other living things:

  • the primary consumer eats plants. Example from the worksheet: the rabbit eats grass. It is a herbivore: it eats only plants.
  • the secondary consumer eats the primary consumer. Example from the worksheet: the fox eats the rabbit. It can be a carnivore (it eats meat) or an omnivore (it eats plants and animals).

When plants and animals die, the decomposers start working: fungi and bacteria. They break them down and put the nutrients back into the soil, and the plants use them to grow. Earthworms and some insects help them. So the circle closes and starts again.

The chain on the worksheet is:

Sun → grass (producer) → rabbit (primary consumer) → fox (secondary consumer) → decomposers → nutrients in the soil → grass

The arrows. The arrows show the direction in which matter and energy move. They point to the one that gets the energy, that is the one that eats. So we write grass → rabbit ("the grass goes into the rabbit's tummy"), never rabbit → grass.

A mountain example, like the ecosystem chosen by your group: Sun → grass → marmot → eagle. The golden eagle really hunts marmots.

Check questions

  1. Why are plants called producers?
  2. What does a primary consumer eat?
  3. Who are the decomposers and what do they do?
  4. Which way do the arrows point in a food chain?
  5. In the chain grass → rabbit → fox, who is the secondary consumer?
Show the answers

Answers: 1. Because they make their own food by photosynthesis, using the light of the Sun. 2. Producers, that is plants. 3. Fungi and bacteria: they break down dead plants and animals and give the nutrients back to the soil. 4. Towards the one that gets the energy, the one that eats. 5. The fox.
