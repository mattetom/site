TO BE and HAVE GOT

Hi! Two very important verbs. Read them, say them out loud, then try page 2.
TO BE = essere

Who you are, how old you are, how you feel, where you are.

I'm nine. She's a teacher. We're at the park.

HAVE GOT = avere

Things, animals, family, hair and eyes.

I've got two rabbits. She's got long hair.

TO BE in three ways

YESaffirmative

I am = I'm
you are = you're
he / she / it is = he's
we are = we're
they are = they're

NOnegative

I'm not
you aren't
he / she / it isn't
we aren't
they aren't

?question

Am I ...?
Are you ...?
Is he / she / it ...?
Are we ...?
Are they ...?

The magic apostrophe '

It takes the place of the letter that goes away.

I am = I'm is not = isn't are not = aren't

Short answers

Are you tired? Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.

Is she tall? Yes, she is. / No, she isn't.

Are they at home? Yes, they are. / No, they aren't.

HAVE GOT

I / you / we / they have got = I've got
he / she / it has got = she's got

I've got two rabbits. My sister has got a bike.

If you have studied it in class:

No: I haven't got a cat. She hasn't got a dog.
?: Have you got a cat? Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.

Watch out!

  • Age: I am nine years old. I have nine years.
  • Rabbits: I have got two rabbits. I am two rabbits. (I'm not a rabbit!)
  • My brother, my mum, my birthday: is. My brother is two.
  • Spell it well: aren't · isn't · I'm · rabbit (two b) · engineer
